What the app does

LabelLight reads the text of food labels with your device’s camera and compares it with a list of words you choose to avoid (allergens, intolerance packs and custom words). It can also look up a barcode in Open Food Facts, an open third-party database. It shows which words matched and whether a “may contain” statement was found.

LabelLight does not test food, does not diagnose or treat any allergy, intolerance or condition, and does not give medical advice. Allergen lists follow the US FALCPA and FASTER Act (9 major food allergens), EU Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 Annex II (14) and Japan’s Food Labeling Standards (8 mandatory items, 特定原材料); intolerance packs flag ingredients by name, without serving sizes or thresholds.

Allergy disclaimer

  • It can miss things. Text recognition can misread or skip words, especially on curved, shiny, small or damaged print. A label can use a word that is not in our list. Database entries can be incomplete, wrong or out of date, and manufacturers change recipes.
  • No result is an all-clear. The best result LabelLight shows is “None of your triggers found in what we read — always check the package.” That means only that the app did not find a match in the text it read.
  • The package and your doctor come first. Always read the label on the package yourself and follow the advice of your doctor or dietitian. If you have a severe allergy, follow your emergency plan.
  • Your choices are yours. You decide which triggers to check. LabelLight checks only what you selected; for example, it follows FDA guidance that coconut is not a tree nut, so coconut is flagged only if you add it as a custom word.
